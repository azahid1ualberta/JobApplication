## Verdict: the best of these three, and a genuinely good fit

This is the one to prioritise. Three things line up that rarely do:

1. **You worked on the Ontario Line.** Your Mott MacDonald placement was traffic simulation of
   how Ontario Line construction staging would affect the surrounding streets. That is the
   question behind every Traffic Management Plan this role will review.
2. **You are the other side of the table.** The job is coordinating with *"local municipalities,
   local road authorities having jurisdictions"* — that is the City of Toronto, and you work
   there, in the transportation division, on road and right-of-way data.
3. **The technical asks are yours.** *"Traffic modelling, traffic impact studies, and traffic
   operations analysis"* — VISSIM and Synchro, plus reviewing the transportation studies filed
   with development applications.

The education bar (engineering, applied science or transportation planning) you clear twice.
P.Eng. is only an **asset** here. There is no supervision requirement.

- **Resume:** City bullets lead with reviewing development applications and their

  The summary names the Ontario Line construction staging work. Metrolinx entry is three

- **Cover letter:** opens on the two facts that make you stand out — municipal side of the
  relationship, and Ontario Line experience. It says honestly that permits are not your own area.

## Before you apply

- **Ask your City colleagues in Right of Way Management** how Ontario Line road occupancy and
  closure requests reach the City and what slows them down. Ten minutes of that is worth more
  in an interview than anything you could read.
- Skim **Ontario Traffic Manual Book 7** (temporary conditions / work zones). It is the
  reference every TMP is written against.
- Know the Ontario Line basics: the southern civil works, the elevated and at-grade sections,
  and where the construction is hardest on local streets.
