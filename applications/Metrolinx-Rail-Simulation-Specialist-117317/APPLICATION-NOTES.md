## This is the closest posting to your actual job title

Your Metrolinx role was **Transportation Planner, Simulation and Modelling**. The posting is
called **Rail Simulation Specialist**. That is not a loose thematic match — it is the same
work under a different label, and OpenTrack is named twice in the qualifications.

Three lines from the posting and where they land:

> *"Responsible for modelling and simulating rail network utilization scenarios using industry
> and best practice standards."*

OpenTrack scenario work. Directly yours.

> *"Communicates and works with stakeholders to review and validate service level increases
> using Open Track software."*

The software is named. You used it daily for a year.

> *"Anticipates and leads the development of infrastructure requirements for service expansion
> growth as well as **construction activities that would reduce capacity** and recommend
> solutions for high-risk areas and issues."*

This is slow-order and possession impact analysis, which was a large part of your Metrolinx
year. It is also the one line where your **Mott MacDonald** placement earns its place — you
simulated how Ontario Line construction staging would affect the surrounding network, which is
the same question asked from the road side. The cover letter's third paragraph makes both
points together.

## Two blockers that hit other postings are absent here

- **No CROR requirement.** Not required, not preferred, not mentioned. Compare 116643, where
  CROR was stated as required twice and I told you to skip the posting.
- **No supervision requirement.** The role "oversees, monitors" work but the qualifications ask
  for no staff supervision at all. The absence of management experience has been the single
  biggest recurring blocker across everything I have built for you — it does not apply here.

The education bar is *"a university degree in engineering, information technology,
transportation planning, economics or a related discipline."* You clear it twice over.

**Note the posting's own line:** *"If you're excited about working with Metrolinx but your past
experience doesn't quite align with every qualification of this posting, we encourage you to
apply."* On a one-year-versus-several-years gap, that sentence is worth taking at face value.

## Note on tone

Same restraint as the Timetabling Specialist documents: neither the Crew Variance Tool nor the
command line workflow is named anywhere. You are writing to Metrolinx again, and your reason
for leaving them out stands — naming tools you built for that organization reads as selling
them their own work back.

What carries the technical capability instead is one subordinate clause on the resume,
*"working from the operations data directly where the volumes ruled out doing it by hand,"*
and, in the letter, the emphasis on what the analysis established rather than how it was
produced. Anyone who knows the work reads what is behind it.

**Save the turnaround numbers for the interview.** In a room they land as recall; on paper,
to this audience, they read as a claim.

## Before you apply — you have five days

- **Apply early in the window, not on 4 October.** Five days is tight and the posting went up
  on 24 September, so the file is already moving.
- Be ready for **"demonstrated years"** to come up. Do not oversell the year. The stronger
  answer is scope: what you simulated, across which subdivisions, and what decisions came out
  of it. Depth against a short tenure is a defensible position; padding is not.
- **Read up on freight interaction on the GO network** — shared corridors, CN and CP host
  agreements, and how freight windows constrain commuter timetables. You have not modelled
  freight, but showing you understand where it bites turns "commuter only" from a gap into a
  boundary you can describe.
- Have **RTC** placed: know what it is, what it is used for in North American practice, and
  say plainly that you would be learning it.

## Priority

**Apply.** Permanent, the right band, in-office at 130 Adelaide, no CROR, no supervision
requirement, and the job title is effectively the one you already held. The one-year tenure is
the only real obstacle, and the posting explicitly invites applications that do not meet every
qualification.
