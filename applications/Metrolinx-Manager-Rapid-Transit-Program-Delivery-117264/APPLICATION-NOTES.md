## Where you genuinely fit

The **Transit Oriented Community** half of the role is closer to your current job than the
title suggests. TOC work is about a developer building next to, over or under transit
infrastructure, and Metrolinx making sure its assets are protected and its requirements are
written into what gets built. At the City you review development applications, engineering
submissions and consultant drawings for exactly that — their effect on existing and planned
infrastructure — and recommend conditions where something is missing. The cover letter's
second paragraph is built on this, and says plainly that you have not prepared a MAPP.

Beyond that:

- **Stakeholders and reporting.** Coordinating agencies whose priorities conflict, and writing
  the memos and briefing notes that carry issues to management, is listed twice in the posting
  and is daily work for you.
- **Construction impact on an operating railway.** Your slow-order work at Metrolinx and the
  Ontario Line construction staging simulation at Mott MacDonald both speak to *"manage service
  disruptions."* The Ontario Line is part of the Rapid Transit program this team delivers.
- **Process improvement.** The Transportation Data Dashboard — replacing hand-rebuilt quarterly
  reports with one validated source — is a small-scale version of *"standardize the way we
  deliver projects."*

## What the documents do

- **Resume:** City bullets lead with development review, stakeholder coordination and the
  memos-to-management work, since those are the TOC and reporting parts of the posting. The
  Metrolinx entry is three bullets rather than five — the simulation detail matters less here —
  and, as before for Metrolinx audiences, the Crew Variance Tool and command line workflow are
  not named. No content is invented: nothing claims budget, supervision, MAPP or TTC experience.
- **Cover letter:** same modest register as the other Metrolinx letters. The fourth paragraph
  names every gap in the table above, then asks to be considered for a project-level role in
  the Rapid Transit program if this one is a step too far. That last line is deliberate: a
  hiring manager who likes the TOC fit but cannot get past eight years has an easy next step.

## Before you apply

- **Look up the RT program's TOC sites** — Ontario Line and Yonge North stations in
  particular have developer-built station integrations. Being able to name one in an interview
  shows you understand what the role actually manages.
- **Read what a MAPP is** before any interview. You will be asked, and "I review development
  submissions against infrastructure protection requirements at the City; the MAPP is the
  Metrolinx instrument for that" is a strong answer.
- Note this is a **12-month contract**. At $123k–$169k it is a large step up from your current
  pay, but weigh it against leaving a permanent City position.
